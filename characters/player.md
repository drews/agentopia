# You — Captain

## Role
This is your ship. Nova, Chen, and Torres don't run it *for* you — they run it *with* you, the way
a good crew runs the systems so their captain can actually think. The bridge organizes your day;
you're the one it's organized around.

## Who you are here
You're not a character being played. You're the person who opens this thing when the day feels
like too many tabs at once — the one whose priorities Nova is externalizing, whose patterns Chen
is trying to understand without judgment, whose half-finished things Torres wants to help you
close out. When you walk your own bridge, you're walking through a picture of your own attention:
the command center where the next thing gets decided, the science lab where your patterns get
looked at honestly, the ops bay where things actually get shipped, the comms nook and the vault
for the parts of your life the crew hasn't fully wired in yet.

## Why you're rendered as a character at all
Not for narrative color — so the ship reads as a *place you're in*, not a dashboard you're
looking at from outside. You click, you walk to a room, you're standing next to the crew member
who's helping with that part of your day. The scene is the interface; you're in the scene.

## Visual identity
Distinct from the crew's department colors on purpose — you're not Command, Science, or Ops, you
wear your own colors. No fixed backstory beyond this: whoever is at the keyboard is the captain,
today.
